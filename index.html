NeurIPS 2026 Workshop

Cognitive Interpretability of Foundation Models

December 2026  •  Paris, France

Building Cognitive Interpretability as the unified science of foundation model cognition, behavior, and internal representations.

About the Workshop

Understanding latent cognitive capabilities, behaviors, and learned internal representations of foundation models has emerged as a central challenge in AI research, with broad implications for the science of intelligence, ML theory, and AI alignment. This workshop provides a dedicated venue for researchers from cognitive science, computational neuroscience, and mechanistic interpretability to build new collaborations and establish common methodological standards under the umbrella of cognitive interpretability.

Cognitive Capabilities of Foundation Models

Foundation models serve as model organisms for the science of intelligence — systems that allow systematic manipulation of task conditions to isolate computational principles driving behavior. Despite exciting recent work, we still lack principled measurement frameworks for foundation models whose cognitive capabilities may fundamentally differ from those of humans.

Understanding Internal Representations

Just as zebra finch models in biology provide access to neural circuits, foundation models expose internal states that can be conveniently experimented with at scale. Tools from representational similarity analysis, neural alignment, and mechanistic interpretability offer a complementary lens on which internal states can be measured.

Topics of Interest

  • Comparing human and LLM-based agents under matched task constraints to characterize similarities and differences in behaviour and representations
  • Extracting latent inductive biases and characterizing emerging cognitive capabilities in foundation models
  • Using foundation models to test theories of cognition at scale
  • Examining human-AI performance gaps as diagnostic signals for representational structure
  • Representational similarity analysis and neural alignment as tools for model interpretation
  • Using computational cognitive models as baselines for evaluating and interpreting foundation model behavior (causal reasoning, abstraction learning)
  • Identifying internal structures that support cognitive-like computations in foundation models
